Based on Adobe Stock’s Contributor Guidelines for Generative AI and Quality and Technical Standards, human moderators and automated inspection algorithms evaluate submissions at 100% (1:1 pixel) zoom.The most common reasons AI images are rejected include:Generative AI issues: Anatomical inaccuracies, malformed structures, unrealistic lighting/shadows, and render artifacts.Quality & Technical issues: Over-sharpening halos, blown highlights, crushed blacks, unnatural saturation, and softness.Intellectual Property: Hallucinated logos or brand motifs.The following visual post-processing adjustments and pipeline "hacks" address these specific rejection triggers:1. Fix the "AI Eyes" and Anatomical TellsRound the Pupils & Add Matching Catchlights: AI models frequently render non-circular pupils (cat-slit or oval shapes) or place the specular reflection (catchlight) at different angles in each eye. Standardize the pupils to perfect circles and copy the reflection highlight from one eye to the other so the light source looks physically unified.Inspect Symmetry in Bilateral Accessories: Glasses frames, ear piercings, collars, and necklace chains are notorious for asymmetrical warping or melting into flesh. Straighten frame bridges and remove asymmetric floating jewelry.Fix Merged Teeth: Generative AI often produces a single, solid white strip instead of individual teeth. Use a fine separation brush with low opacity to trace subtle tooth dividers, or slightly darken the interdental spaces.2. Clean Upscaler Halos and Edge FringingDefringe High-Contrast Edges: When upscaling with models like UltraSharp, high-contrast borders (e.g., dark hair or jackets against bright skies) develop a 1–2 pixel wide white "glow" or neon purple/green chromatic aberration. In Photoshop, GIMP, or via code (OpenCV/Pillow):Apply a Defringe / Remove Color Aberration filter.Use a clone stamp or a small 1px median blur restricted strictly to high-contrast edges to eliminate over-sharpening halos.Clean the "Blur Bleed" Boundary: Diffusion models often blur the background, but the blur will unnaturally "spill" 5–10 pixels into the sharp edges of foreground objects. Sharpening or masking that boundary so the in-focus subject cuts cleanly against the blurred background satisfies Adobe’s requirement for realistic depth-of-field.3. Dynamic Range & Color Balance HacksClamp Extreme Highlights and Shadows (Defeat "Over/Underexposure"):Stock reviewers reject images with completely blown-out whites (#FFFFFF) or clipped crushed blacks (#000000).The Fix: In your Levels/Curves adjustment, clamp the output black point up to 4 or 5 and pull the highlight white point down to 250 or 252. This preserves detail in deep shadows and bright skies, mimicking an actual camera sensor’s dynamic range roll-off.Desaturate the "AI Skin Cast":Many AI generators oversaturate reds and magentas, giving people a sunburned, wax-figure look. Lower the saturation of the Red and Orange color channels by 5% to 10%, and nudge the warmth toward natural olive/neutral tones.Tone Down Neon Greens: Foliage, grass, and trees in generative models often look neon or radioactive. Desaturate the Yellow and Green channels slightly to match natural environmental lighting.4. The "Sensor Grain" Texture HackGenerative AI and neural upscalers create perfectly flat, waxy surfaces on skin, walls, and skies that scream "synthetic" to inspection algorithms.The Fix: Add a uniform layer of monochromatic 35mm sensor noise:Set noise amount to 0.6% – 1.2% (Monochromatic / Gaussian).Blend it over the entire 4K image.Why this works:It instantly masks minor upscaler artifacts, checkerboard dithering, and JPEG compression banding.It gives the automated reviewer the spatial frequency profile of a real high-ISO DSLR/Mirrorless camera sensor rather than an AI paint pass.5. Remove Hallucinated IP and Gibberish TextScrub "Pseudo-Logos": AI models tend to hallucinate brand-like insignia on shoes (fake Nike/Adidas swooshes/stripes), laptop lids (fake Apple motifs), camera lenses, car front grilles, and polo shirts. Adobe's moderation will reject these under IP/Trademark Issues even if the logo is fictional. Spot-heal or stamp over every emblem, button pattern, or badge.Erase Background Text: Background signs, billboards, storefronts, and book spines almost always feature illegible, wavy gibberish. Replace them with blank solid textures or clone background scenery over them.6. Technical Export Requirements ChecklistWhen exporting the final file from your pipeline:Format: JPEG only. (PNG submissions for regular stock photos are rejected or restricted).Quality Setting: Export at maximum quality (100% / Level 12 / Subsampling 4:4:4). Do not compress to save space; Adobe allows up to 45 MB per file.Color Profile: Ensure the image is explicitly converted to sRGB (sRGB IEC61966-2.1). Submitting in Adobe RGB or Display P3 can cause color-space mismatch during Adobe's web rendering, resulting in a dull, muddy preview that fails aesthetic moderation.Target Resolution: Adobe requires images to be at least 4 Megapixels (MP) and no more than 100 MP. A 4K image (e.g., $3840 \times 2160 \approx 8.3 \text{ MP}$ or $4096 \times 4096 \approx 16.7 \text{ MP}$) hits the target resolution for stock commercial licensing.
